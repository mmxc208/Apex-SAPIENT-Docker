Goat step 4b: the host on its own machine

Work of 2026-11-19 on branch goat-step4b-host-vm. The host fusion node and the host recorder moved to a VM of their own, and eleven checks were run on three machines. Every number on this page comes from docs/acceptance-goat4b.md or from the evidence files it names in docs/evidence/goat4b/. All times are UTC. One file, no network needed.

Why step 4b

Step 4 put the step 1 rig on the real goatnet cuaslab. It ran the pod, the host fusion node and the host recorder in one VM, Colima. So the two fusion nodes shared one clock, one Docker engine and one route to the fabric router.

Three things could not be tested that way. The first is a clock difference between the pod and the host. The second is a cut of the real network link. The third is a host that loses its own link.

Step 4b moved the host fusion node and the host recorder to a new VM, goathost. The pod stayed in Colima. The goatnet stayed in its own VM, goatlite. Now pod, goatnet and host are on three machines, as in a deployment. Then eleven checks, G4B-01 to G4B-11, were run.

The programs did not change. Step 4b added a compose overlay, helper scripts and documents (check G4B-10).

The main results

  • Placement. The two host services run only in goathost. The pod's key is not there.
  • No direct path. Colima and goathost could not reach each other. Everything between pod and host crosses the fabric router. This rests on how the Mac's virtual network behaves, not on a rule of the rig.
  • A task, hop by hop. One host task was seen at every hop to the pod fusion node and back. With the bridge stopped, none reached the pod.
  • Clocks. With no clock touched, the pod's messages reached the host a few milliseconds old, and nothing was rejected.
  • Clock tolerance. The host's clock was set off by hand. The host rejects the pod's messages once its clock is more than 0.1 s behind the pod's, or more than 0.9 s ahead: these limits are from the validator's window in source. The steps bracket them: accepted at -0.05 and +0.8 s, rejected at -0.15 and +1.2 s.
  • Link cuts. With its link cut, the pod went on working. After a long cut the host removed the pod and accepted it again. When the host's own link was cut, its connection came back by itself, with no restart.

What was not run, or is unknown

  • The removal of the VM was never run.
  • Seven blocks of the README were run as written, as one zsh -f script with stdin at /dev/null; the others were not. Its own record of which blocks ran is in the "Left over" tab.
  • In G4B-06, two cells are unknown: the host's messages at the pod at -0.15 and -0.30 s. Three more are not judged.
  • Only the host's clock was stepped, never the pod's.
  • Each check ran once, for minutes, on one Mac.

The "What was tested" tab gives each check with what it measured and where it falls short.

Three words used on this page

Apparent age. The receiver's clock when a message arrives, minus the time the sender wrote into the message. It is the time in transit plus the difference between the two clocks. The two parts cannot be told apart from the age alone.

The window. Each fusion node checks every message it receives. It accepts the message only if its apparent age is between -0.1 s and 0.9 s. Otherwise it drops the message and logs invalid message from ... dropped. The limits are read from the source, emulators/fake_fusion_node.py.

Blackhole route. A route that tells a machine's kernel to drop every packet to one address, without an answer. The link cuts of this step replaced the normal route to the fabric router by one.

Two clocks

In step 4 both fusion nodes ran in one VM and read one clock. In step 4b the pod reads Colima's clock and the host reads the clock of goathost. Each fusion node checks the apparent age of every message it receives against the window, -0.1 s to 0.9 s. So a difference between the two clocks can make one side drop the other's messages.

Apparent age is the receiver's clock on arrival minus the sender's time in the message. A negative age means the message looks as if it came from the future.

The natural difference (G4B-05)

First, no clock was touched. Each VM kept its own time, through its own time services. The host recorder logs, for each message of the pod, the time it arrived. So the apparent age of every pod message at the host could be read.

712pod messages since host-fusion started, read at 12:41
0.007 stheir median apparent age; the largest was 0.022 s
0outside the window, and 0 rejections in both fusion nodes' logs

A later window, 12:59:25 to 13:04:44, after more than 5 minutes of traffic: 387 pod messages, ages from -0.002 to 0.013 s, median 0.002 s. 41 of them looked as if from the future, by up to 2 ms. The host's own messages, made and recorded on one clock, had a median of 0.003 s. Again 0 rejections.

What it shows. In that window the two clocks agreed to within a few milliseconds, transit included. The ages cannot separate a clock difference from the time in transit.

The VMs' offsets from the Mac were also read through ssh, 30 rounds each. Each offset lies close to its bound (for example goathost +20.4 ms, bound ±26.6 ms), so they say little. One Mac gives all three VMs their clock source, which is not the case in a deployment.

The clock set on purpose (G4B-06)

The script stopped the two time services of goathost. Then it stepped the VM's clock to each offset in turn, waited 5 s and read a window of 40 s.

The sign. A negative offset means the host's clock is behind, so the pod's messages look as if from the future at the host.

Each step is judged by a rule: it passes when every age is inside the window and none was rejected, or every age is outside and none was accepted. With ages on both sides, or with fewer than 20 messages, the step is not judged.

Offset of the host's clock:

What it means for a deployment

The host rejects the pod's messages once its clock is more than 0.1 s behind the pod's, or more than 0.9 s ahead. These two limits are from the validator's window in source, not measured. The steps bracket them: the host accepted the pod's messages at -0.05 and +0.8 s, and rejected all of them at -0.15 and +1.2 s. With no clock touched, on these three VMs, nothing was rejected.

The other direction has its own limit. The pod drops the host's messages once they look as if from the future by more than 0.1 s, that is once the host's clock is more than 0.1 s ahead. That limit is inferred from the window; it was not measured below +0.5 s. At +1.2 s the pod dropped all 45 of the host's messages. At +0.5 and +0.8 s both of the 2 tasks in each window were dropped; too few to judge.

The ages in the steps are the offset plus a base age of about 0.006 s, measured before the first step.

Back to normal

After the last step the script moved the clock back by 1.2 s and started both time services again. The offset was 26.7 ms after the restore against 25.4 ms before the step. In the next 60 s the host accepted 70 of 70 pod messages, with a median age of 0.006 s, as at the base. No container was restarted: StartedAt: unchanged for all 10 containers.

Before the run, the script was killed on purpose during the step to -0.15 s. Its trap put the clock back and the gate passed afterwards (task-4-trap-demo.txt).

Where it falls short

  • One run, 40 s per offset.
  • The down direction is judged at one offset of six. The host sends only two tasks in 40 s. The pod logs no age for a message it accepts.
  • Most of the down traffic at -0.15, -0.30 and +1.2 s was the host's Errors. They exist only because the host rejected the pod's messages.
  • Only the clock of goathost was stepped, never the pod's.
  • The run scripts have no unit tests.

Cutting the real link

In step 4 a cut took the bridge's container off a Docker network. In step 4b a cut is a blackhole route: the route to the fabric router, 192.168.64.4, is replaced by one that drops every packet. Each script read the route first, refused to run unless it was the normal one, and put back exactly the line it read.

Times are as logged, in whole seconds unless shown. On each line, orange marks are the pod's, teal marks the host's and dark marks the route's. The shaded band is the cut.

  • During the cut the pod logged 0 lines with lost or cannot reach. Its fusion node logged 6 detection lines: the pod went on working.
  • In the 25 s after, the host logged 2 detection lines and 0 new registrations.
  • The route was back as before: 192.168.64.4 via 192.168.64.1 dev col0.

G4B-07, long: the pod's link cut for 40 s, host hold time 20 s

The host forgets a node it has not heard from for its hold time. The hold time is 120 s by default. For this cut the host services were made again with a hold time of 20 s, so that a cut of 40 s would pass it.

G4B-08: the host's own link cut for 30 s

Here the route in goathost was cut, not the pod's. Before this run it was not known what the host would do.

  • The host recorder held 0 records of the pod from shortly after the cut until the restore. The first came 0.9 s after the restore.
  • The first logged detection line came 7 s after the restore (the host logs every 10th).
  • No new registration. Both host containers kept their start time: StartedAt unchanged: yes.

Falls short: one cut of 30 s. After the restore the pod's records came back at the normal rate, with no burst. So the messages of the cut were lost, not delivered later. This is read from counts by 5 s, not message by message. Evidence: task-4-g4b-08.txt.

How step 4b fits goat's ADRs

Step 4b is about how real the test is. It does not make the rig conform to more of goat's architecture decisions (ADRs). It changes none of the four points where the rig does not conform.

VerdictWhat
meetsThe host programs connect directly to the fabric router. Each has its own slot, that is its own fabric identity. A slot writes only below its own anchor.
partlyTask authority: who may task the pod is decided only by the bridge's allowlist, not by the router.
not usedNeither the ingress gateway (ADR 1020) nor the edge router (ADR 1157). The pod is enrolled in this goatnet with its authority and connects to the router as a client. The edge router is the case of step 6, the external host.

The four points that do not conform

PointADRChanged by 4b?
The pod's machine is not a mesh peer.1092No
Nothing can cut a machine off.1025, 1031No
The sensors and the pod fusion node have no fabric identity.1122No
Every source appears as the pod.1251No

Why the first two cannot be met on a goatlite

On a goatlite the router listens on the LAN, and the box is not a mesh peer. So the pod reaches the router over the LAN, not over the mesh.

goat cuts a machine off by removing it from the mesh, because Zenoh has no list of revoked certificates. A machine that is not on the mesh cannot be cut off that way.

The last two need the carriage rules of ADR 1251, which are proposed and not built. More: docs/goat-context.md gap 14 and sections 2.7 and 4.1, and the page docs/explainers/goat-hierarchy/index.html.

How to remove it

None of this was run. It is read from the source of goat4b/host-vm.sh.

  1. From the repository root: . goat4/dg4.sh; . goat4b/host-vm.sh; . goat4b/dg4b.sh
  2. dg4b_host down and dg4b_pod down
  3. g4b_vm_remove --yes. It removes the route and the route file on goatlite, then stops and deletes goathost with its disk. Without --yes it prints what it would remove and changes nothing.

Nothing needs to be removed from Colima or from cuaslab. The record also gives the steps by hand.

  • G4B-06, two unknown cells: the host's messages at the pod at -0.15 and -0.30 s. The pod accepted all 49 each time, but logs no age for an accepted message.
  • The pod's clock was never stepped, only the host's.
  • The fallback engine path (G4B_ENGINE=shell) was never used in a run.
  • The traps that restore a route were not shown by an interrupted run. The clock trap was.

Known limits that stay

  • One Mac. The three VMs share its clock source and its network stack, and it routes every packet between them.
  • The closed path between pod and host comes from the Mac's virtual network, not from the rig. A route added through the Mac would open it, and nothing checks that it stays closed.
  • The keys. The Mac holds all three keys. Step 4b keeps the pod's key out of goathost; the two host keys are now on the Mac and on the disk of goathost.
  • Reading is not limited on cuaslab, and every limit of step 4 that concerns the programs remains.
  • Two helpers, one project. dg4 of step 4 and dg4b_pod act on the same project. Nothing stops dg4 up -d while the step 4b rig runs.
