## Differences from step 1

From the design, section 6.5, and what the runs showed:

- **The router** is `cuaslab`'s, `zenohd v1.9.0-modified`, not our stock `eclipse/zenoh:1.9.0`. We do not restart it, so G4-01 has no restart case, and we do not read its log.
- **The identities** come from the `cuaslab` portal: three machines, one slot each. The CA is `cuaslab goatlite CA`. In step 1 the generator made a test CA and a slot pool of 8.
- **Access.** A refused write is shown by absence at a subscriber with a control write, not by a router log line (G4-05). Every TLS client may read `**` on `cuaslab`, so the step 1 case "a subscription to `**` is refused" (part of G1-05) does not apply. In step 1 the stand-in router had goat's scoped read on.
- **The goat client** is the released `goat` 0.5.19 on the Mac with the founder's identity and a real context, not a build from source with a hand-written profile (G4-10).
- **The regression target** of G4-11 is the step 1 rig. G4-12 and G4-13 are new.
- **The path.** The rig and the goatnet are two VMs on one Mac, joined by a route on each VM through the Mac, with no WireGuard. The pod's network `pod1` is internal, so the pod bridge reaches the router only through `mesh` (G4-06 pre-check).
- **The bridge noticed the short outage** 5 s after the time printed before the disconnect, against 9 to 10 s in step 1 (G4-06). Not explained.

## What happened that the plan did not foresee

1. The first enrolment was refused with HTTP 500, code `mint`, because the lite preset turns bundle-create on and the binary is not on the box (see "Enrolment"). A goatlite stood up at goatcore `e44e893d` cannot enrol a machine until the setting is turned off or the binary is installed. This is a gap in goat to report upstream; nothing was filed.
2. The portal setting of `cuaslab` was changed, by Richard's decision, against the design's non-goal "no portal setting". The goatcore checkout that deploys `cuaslab` was fast-forwarded from `e44e893d` to `b454a0b5` for the redeploy.
3. Review found two defects in the enrolment script before the live run, and they were fixed: it expected HTTP 200 where the portal answers 201, and it took the first other `*.pem` of a bundle as the root, which in a real bundle is a public key.
4. Task 2's read-only pre-checks (`whoami`, an empty machine list, the router reachable) were made and not saved to a file.
5. G4-13: the plan's method cannot reach 50 ms for Colima, because `colima ssh` takes 192 ms at best.
6. The step 1 pass rule "a subscription to `**` is refused" (part of G1-05) does not apply on `cuaslab`.

## Known limits

From the design, section 9, with what the runs added:

- **No WireGuard, no mesh.** The path to the router is a route between two VMs on one Mac. On a goatnet with a mesh the pod's host would hold class `POD` in the mesh and reach the router through it (ADR 1092, design section 9).
- **Nothing revokes a slot certificate on this path.** goat's cut-off is mesh revocation, and the rig is not on the mesh. The three certificates are valid to 2027-11-18 (from the session).
- **No ingress gateway.** The pod bridge connects to the core router. goat's moon pod contract names an ingress gateway; a goatlite has none (`docs/goat-context.md` 3.4 and gap 13).
- **One Mac.** The pod, the host clients and the goatnet are on one Mac. The host clients run in the same VM as the pod. The clients connect to the router by address; its certificate holds that address (design section 2).
- **Reading is not limited on `cuaslab`.** Every TLS client may subscribe, query and declare a liveliness subscriber on `**` (design section 2). Any of the three slots, and the founder, can read below any anchor.
- **Clocks.** The difference between the two VMs is known only to within 124 ms (G4-13). The fusion nodes' validator accepts a message only within 0.9 s behind and 0.1 s ahead of their clock (`docs/acceptance-goat1.md`, known limits). In this rig both fusion nodes run in the Colima VM, so the SAPIENT timestamps are made and checked on one clock; the goatlite's clock would matter if the router changed timestamps, and goat's router feature for that is not deployed on any goatnet (`docs/goat-context.md` gap 8). On a goatnet where the host runs on another machine, a difference near 100 ms would matter for messages that look as if from the future. No rejection was seen.
- **One slot for the whole pod.** The pod bridge holds one slot for the pod fusion node's traffic. goatcore's ADR 1251 ("every source keeps its identity through a pod") and `docs/design/pod-carriage-and-provenance.md`, in goatcore up to `b454a0b5`, may bear on this; they were not read.
- **The node class of the recorder.** `host-recorder` is enrolled as `FUSION` because no class describes a reader that writes nothing. This is a gap in goat's vocabulary, not a fit (design section 6.2).
- **All the limits of `docs/acceptance-goat1.md` that concern the programs remain**, because no program changed: node ID ownership by first key, the host's unbounded state, detections from an unregistered node, a lost link noticed late, and the others listed there.
- **The scale of the evidence.** One run of each check, of minutes, on one Mac.
